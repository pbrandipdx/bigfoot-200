    Reference

    Monitors — thresholds, not goals

    Descent HR gap. Average HR on sustained descent vs sustained climb, on any long day. Baseline 15–20 bpm; the fatigued pattern is ~2 bpm. Under 10 bpm on a day that should feel comfortable means under-recovery. Two in a row, cut the week.

    Aerobic decoupling. Pace-to-HR, first half vs second half of long days. Target under 5%. Above 8% consistently means the day was too hard or you're under-recovered.

    Zone discipline. Long days sit 125–140 bpm. More than 15% of a long day above 150 and it was a workout, not a long day.

    VO2 max — monitor, do not chase. 42.0 at baseline; expect 43–46 by Bighorn as a byproduct. It will likely decline in Blocks 4 and 5, which is normal. For a 105-hour race it's close to irrelevant.

    HRV — trend only. 30-day rolling baseline. Single low day: ignore. 7-day average down >10%: cut volume 30% that week. "Unbalanced" 7+ days: three full days off.

    Resting HR. +5 bpm sustained over 5 days: back off. +8 bpm: stop and check for illness.

    Sleep. Train rested, race deprived. 7+ hr/night through Blocks 1–4; sleep score 75+ the two nights before any long day.

    Body Battery. Morning of a long day: 70+. Under 50, downgrade the session. Overnight recharge under 30 for three straight nights means accumulated fatigue.

    Hill score / endurance score / running tolerance. Hill score should rise through every block — flat or falling during a climbing block means the vert isn't being absorbed. Running tolerance is the injury guardrail: weekly load stays under it.

    Non-negotiables
    • Night hours accumulate on schedule. Zero so far.
    • Bighorn is a gate, not a formality. Finish well and Bigfoot is on. DNF or a badly damaged finish and the correct decision is to defer — decide the rule now, while it's abstract.
    • Descent training happens on rock, not loam, and late in the day.
    • Feet get managed from hour five, not hour nine. Practice in training.
    • Eat every 40 minutes on every long day, including when you don't want to.
    Review cadence — end of each block
    • Did time on feet, vertical, and peak day hit?
    • Did the descent HR gap hold?
    • Did night hours accumulate?
    • What did hill score and endurance score do?
    • Did the checkpoint race meet its pace target?

    Three or more misses means the next block gets rebuilt, not stacked on top.

    How the numbers are built
    • Time on feet — moving time from Walk, Hike, Run and Stair-Stepper activities. Weight training, swims and ball sports are excluded.
    • Vertical — Strava elevation gain, converted to feet. Treadmill and stair-stepper work reports zero gain, so real climbing is understated whenever the vert came from a machine.
    • Miles — Walk, Hike and Run distance only.
    • Longest effort — the single longest activity in the week, not the daily total.
    • Weeks run Monday to Sunday.
