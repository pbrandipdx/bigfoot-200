## The numbers they give

| | Their guidance | This plan |
|---|---|---|
| Build length | 12–16 weeks (Holz); ~6 months (vert.run) | **101 weeks, 9 blocks** — 2027 is an apprenticeship year, 2028 is the build |
| Weekly progression | **no more than 10%/week** (Holz) | block-to-block jumps of 13–20% |
| Peak weekly volume | ~1.4–1.6× sustainable baseline (vert.run) | **22–24 hr/wk** vs an 8.8 hr measured baseline |
| Minimum for a 100 | 9 hr/wk for 6 consecutive weeks (Koop) | already exceeded |
| Longest single run | no magic number; 4 hr → 8 hr over 16 weeks in Koop's worked example | **20 hr** |
| Back-to-backs | Sat 3–6 hr + Sun 2–4 hr, with pack, conversational (Holz); monthly to every 2 months (Burt) | 2–3 per block ✓ |
| Weekly mileage ceiling | keep under 100 mi/wk (Burt) | 55 → 75 ✓ |
| Taper | 2–3 weeks (vert.run); start ~1 month out (Burt) | ~2–3 weeks ✓ |
| Rest | **one forced rest day per week**; 7–9 hr sleep | ✓ |
| Sleep deprivation | small doses only (Holz, Burt); vert.run explicitly warns against reproducing severe sleep loss | 2 sessions on <4 hr sleep in Block 4 |

## Where this plan is sound

- Hours not miles as the primary metric — both Koop and Burt insist on it
- Back-to-back frequency and structure
- Taper length
- Race ladder, and BURT 100 eight weeks before Bigfoot clears Burt's warning
  against a 100-miler within a month of a 200
- The night-hours progression (3 → 6 → 12 → 20 → 30+) is **more structured than
  anything published** — every source says "practice at night" and none says how much

## Where it diverges, in order of consequence

1. **Peak volume is roughly double the guideline for the measured baseline.**
   vert.run's rule gives 12–14 hr/wk from an 8.8 hr base; the plan asks 22–24.
2. **The 20-hour peak day has no support anywhere.** Koop's worked progression
   tops out near 8 hours. Burt prefers back-to-backs specifically because they
   cut injury risk versus one enormous effort.
3. **The peak block sits 8–10 weeks after a 100-miler**, with real recovery — this was 3–5 weeks in the 2027 plan and is one of the things the move to 2028 fixed.
4. **No scheduled down weeks in Blocks 2–4** — 27 consecutive build weeks.
   Holz's 10%/week cap and planned deloads both point the other way.

## Workouts worth stealing from Holz

These are specific and directly address gaps in the current plan.

- **Sustained downhill: 10–30 minutes continuous.** The single most relevant
  session here — Bigfoot has more descent than climb, and Block 1's stated
  question is about descending without wrecking quads. Nothing in the current
  week does this deliberately.
- **Uphill tempo intervals, progressed:** wk1 3×5 min, wk2 3×10 min, wk3 2×15 min.
  Gives the LeBron ramp work a structure instead of a fixed 10–12 reps.
- **Power hill strides:** 5×20 sec at ~85% on a moderate grade.
- **Rucks:** 60–120 min brisk hiking with a pack 5–10 lb *heavier* than race
  weight. Already doing this — the 30 lb vest walks — worth making deliberate.
- **Strength focus:** glutes/hips, core, single-leg control, posterior chain;
  step-ups with a pack and loaded carries as the ultra-specific options.
- **Fuelling target: up to 120 g carbohydrate per hour**, rehearsed on long days.
  The current plan says "eat every 40 minutes" but sets no carb number.
- **One forced rest day per week; 7–9 hr sleep**, more in heavy blocks.

Holz's framing is that training builds evidence you can point to rather than
motivation you have to summon — which is the same logic as the benchmark repeat
of Elk/Kings on Nov 28.
